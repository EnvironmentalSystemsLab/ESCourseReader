CHAPTER 08

Building heat balance.

A building is a thermal system with heat inputs and outputs. Adding up the U-values of Chapter 07, the air that leaks through the envelope, the heat of people and equipment and the sun through the windows gives the heating power a building needs, and, with degree days, its heating energy over a season.

Steady state throughout; metric units; equations 8.1–8.8. The weather is the Ithaca TMYx file of Chapter 01, the irradiance on windows comes from Chapter 05. US (ASHRAE) and German (DIN V 4108-6, VDI 3807) conventions are given side by side.

01

GAINS = LOSSES · EQ. 8.1

The energy balance of a building

Indoors stays at a constant temperature only if what comes in equals what goes out. In winter the sun and the occupants supply part of the heat; the heating supplies the rest. In summer the balance runs the other way, and the surplus must be removed by ventilation or cooling.

steady heat balance in the heating seasonΦh+Φi+Φs=ΦT+ΦV=(HT+HV)·(θi−θe)lecture: Qi + Qs = Qv + Qc W(8.1)
  • H = HT + HV is the building's heat loss per kelvin: one number that collects the whole envelope and its airtightness. The resistor analogy of Chapter 07 continues: the building is a set of conductances in parallel between the indoor and the outdoor node.
  • For a design heat load (sizing the heating plant) take a cold design temperature, no sun and usually no internal gains: Φh = H · (θi − θe,design). For energy over a season, sum the balance over time (section 06).

03

ACH · BLOWER DOOR · EQ. 8.3

Infiltration and ventilation

Every cubic metre of cold air that replaces warm indoor air must be heated. Infiltration is unwanted air leakage through cracks and joints, driven by wind and buoyancy; ventilation is the fresh air the occupants need. Both are counted the same way.

ventilation heat loss coefficient for an air change rate n (h⁻¹) in a volume VHV=ρ·c·n·V3600≈0.33·n·Vρ = 1.2 kg/m³, c = 1000 J/(kg·K); German texts use 0.34 (air at 20 °C) W/K(8.3)
ExampleThe house in Ithaca: V = 420 m³, n = 1 h⁻¹ (lecture frames 654–656)
HV1.2 × 1000 × 1 × 420 / 3600140 W/K
compareHT of the whole envelope118 W/K
Air change rate n · ACH
Volume of air exchanged per hour divided by the room volume. Leaky existing buildings: 1–2 h⁻¹; tight new buildings: 0.2–0.3 h⁻¹ of infiltration (lecture frame 652). Hygienic fresh-air demand is about 25–30 m³/h per person, roughly 0.3–0.5 h⁻¹ in dwellings.
Blower-door test · n50
A fan holds the building at 50 Pa and measures the air flow (ISO 9972, ASTM E779). Requirements: Germany (GEG, DIN 4108-7) n50 ≤ 3.0 h⁻¹, or ≤ 1.5 h⁻¹ with a ventilation system; IECC in the US: ≤ 3 ACH50 in climate zones 3–8 (Ithaca: 6A); Passivhaus ≤ 0.6 h⁻¹. Under natural conditions roughly n ≈ n50 / 20.
Heat recovery
A balanced mechanical ventilation system with a heat exchanger returns 75–90 % of the heat of the exhaust air: HV × (1 − ηHR). Opening windows cannot.

Airtightness and ventilation are separate tasks: "build tight, ventilate right". Do not reduce the fresh air for hygiene and moisture to save heat (Lehrbuch der Bauphysik II 4.3); reduce the leakage and recover the heat instead.

PEOPLE · LIGHTS · EQUIPMENT

Internal gains

People, lights and appliances turn almost all the energy they use into heat inside the building. In winter this offsets heating; in offices and schools it is often the largest cooling load.

  • German monthly methods use a mean internal gain of 5 W/m² of floor area for dwellings (DIN V 4108-6), about 22 kWh/(m²a) over a 185-day heating season.
  • Only sensible heat raises the air temperature. Latent heat (evaporation from people, cooking, showers) adds moisture; it matters for dehumidification and cooling, not for the winter heat balance.

U · SHGC (g) · LOW-E · EQ. 8.4

Windows: heat loss and solar gain

A window loses heat like a wall, only five to twenty times faster, and it lets the sun in. Two numbers describe it: the U-value for conduction, convection and long-wave radiation, and the solar heat gain coefficient for short-wave radiation.

Whole window Uw
Uw = (Ug·Ag + Uf·Af + ψg·lg) / Aw (ISO 10077-1; Bauphysik Gl. 5.2). Frames: timber and PVC about 1.0–1.5, aluminium without a thermal break up to 4–6 W/(m²K); the spacer at the glass edge adds ψg ≈ 0.03–0.08 W/(m·K). US ratings (NFRC) give the whole-window U-factor and SHGC directly.
SHGC and g
Both are the share of incident solar radiation that ends up as heat in the room: transmitted plus the absorbed part released inward. The EN 410 g-value is usually quoted for the glass at normal incidence; NFRC SHGC for the whole window. Both fall at large angles of incidence (lecture frame 673).
Low-e coatings
A thin metal-oxide layer with ε ≈ 0.02–0.1 cuts the radiation across the gap (Chapter 07, Eq. 7.10). Argon or krypton reduce conduction. On surface #2 a selective coating also rejects solar infrared: low SHGC for cooling. On #3 it keeps a high SHGC for passive solar heating.

Is a window a heat loss or a heater? Over a heating season the answer depends on orientation and glazing. The drawing compares the conduction loss of 1 m² of glass with its solar gain for each month of the Ithaca file.

HDD · Gt20/15 · EQ. 8.5–8.6

Degree days and seasonal heating demand

In steady state the heat loss is H × Δθ. Over a season Δθ changes every hour, but because the balance is linear we only need its sum: how much, and for how long, the outdoor temperature stayed below a base temperature. That sum is the degree-day number.

heating degree days from daily mean temperatures, and degree hours from hourly onesHDD=∑days(θbase−θe,mean)+,DH=∑hours(θbase−θe)+( )⁺: only positive values count K·d, K·h(8.5)
seasonal heat loss from the heat loss coefficientQ=H·HDD·24 h/dW/K × K·d × 24 h/d = Wh; divide by 1000 for kWh(8.6)

  • The base is not the thermostat setting: gains supply the last few kelvin, so heating starts below the balance point θb (Eq. 8.7). A well-insulated house has a lower balance point and far fewer degree days.
  • The lecture used 4150 K·d at 18 °C from a Syracuse weather file. The Ithaca TMYx file (built from 2009–2023) gives 3484 K·d at 18 °C; 4150 is reached only at a base near 20 °C. Always state the base, the convention and the weather file with a degree-day value.
  • German practice combines degree days and gains in one line: Qh = 66 · (HT + HV) − 0.95 · (Qs + Qi) kWh/a, where 66 = 2.9 kK·d × 24 h × 0.95 / 1000 (EnEV 2002 annual method; Bauphysik Gl. 10.60).
  • Cooling degree days are far less reliable: cooling depends on solar and internal gains and on humidity at least as much as on outdoor temperature (Szokolay). Use them only as an indicator, and hourly simulation for design.
  • Heating energy is useful energy. The fuel or electricity delivered to the site is larger by the losses of the system (boiler efficiency η ≈ 0.9–0.95) or smaller with a heat pump (COP 2.5–4); primary energy adds the losses of producing and delivering it (lecture frame "Energy ≠ Energy").

Useful gains and thermal mass

On mild, sunny days the gains exceed the losses and the surplus overheats the rooms instead of saving heating. Monthly methods (ISO 13790, DIN V 4108-6, DIN V 18599) multiply the gains by a utilisation factor that depends on the gain-to-loss ratio γ and on the building's time constant τ = C/H (Chapter 07, Eq. 7.13):

utilisation factor of the gainsη=1−γa1−γa+1,γ=Qi+QsQT+QV,a=1+τ16 hQh = QT + QV − η · (Qi + Qs) (Bauphysik Gl. 10.34–10.40)(8.8)
  • At γ = 0.5 a light building (τ = 8 h) uses 79 % of its gains, a heavy one (τ = 48 h) 97 %; at γ = 1 the values are 60 % and 80 %. Mass matters most where gains are large: passive solar houses, offices, spring and autumn.
  • This is where transient behaviour (Chapter 07, section 09) enters a steady method. For daily peaks and overheating, use an hourly simulation.

Model assumptions and precision

Steady state, one well-mixed zone at θi. Degree days use daily means of the 24 hourly values of the TMYx file. Window balances use centre-of-glass U and SHGC, a vertical unshaded window, the isotropic sky of Chapter 05 with ground reflectance 0.2, and no angle dependence of the SHGC; gains are not reduced by a utilisation factor. The house lab treats the floor as adiabatic, as in the lecture, and counts 100 W per person (sensible plus latent); seasonal demand uses the degree days at the balance point of the residents' and equipment gains and omits solar gains.
